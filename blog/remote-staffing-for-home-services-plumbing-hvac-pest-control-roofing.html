Remote staffing for home services: which roles to hire first

For plumbing, HVAC, pest control, roofing, and cleaning companies: the office roles that work best remote, in the order most owners should fill them.

Home services companies are the best fit we have found for remote office staff, and it is not close. The techs are in the field. The owner is in a truck half the day. The office is a phone, a calendar, and a pile of invoices. All three of those travel fine over the internet.

Here is the order we recommend filling roles, based on what has worked for our partners in pest control, plumbing, HVAC, roofing, and cleaning.

1. Customer support representative (answer the phone)

Missed calls are missed jobs. The first remote hire for almost every home services company should be someone whose whole job is answering the phone and the inbox fast, booking the job, and following up on quotes. This one role usually pays for itself in the first month because booked jobs go up and the owner stops answering at 9pm.

What to look for: clear English, calm under pressure, comfortable with your field service software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, FieldRoutes, and the like). Hybryd screens for all three.

2. Dispatcher or route manager

Once calls are handled, the next bottleneck is getting techs to the right jobs in the right order. A remote dispatcher watches the board all day, moves jobs when a tech runs long, confirms tomorrow's schedule the night before, and calls customers when someone is running late. Pest control companies with recurring routes get a lot out of this role; so do HVAC companies in peak season.

3. Accounts receivable and billing

Invoices that go out late get paid late. A remote AR clerk sends invoices the same day the job closes, chases past-dues on a schedule, posts payments, and keeps QuickBooks clean. Roofing and construction companies, where jobs are bigger and progress billing is common, usually feel this one most.

4. Office manager

When you have two or three remote team members and a few techs, you need someone running the office rather than just working in it. A remote office manager owns scheduling, vendor orders, permit paperwork, tech timesheets, reviews, and the hundred small things that used to land on the owner. This is the hire that gives owners their evenings back.

5. Marketing coordinator

Reviews, Google Business Profile, social posts, email reminders for seasonal services, and keeping the website current. Not a strategist, a doer. Most home services companies do not need an agency; they need one consistent person doing the basics every week.

Why remote works here specifically

  • None of these roles need to touch a tool or a truck.
  • Your busy hours are predictable, and our team members work your hours.
  • The software is already cloud-based at most companies.
  • The local labor market for these roles is thin and turns over fast. A full-time remote professional who stays three years changes the business.

Questions we get about this

Yes. Any VoIP system (RingCentral, Dialpad, Nextiva, Grasshopper, the phone features in ServiceTitan or Jobber) works from anywhere with a login and a headset.

What about after-hours and weekend coverage?

Hybryd places full-time team members on your schedule. If you need evening or weekend coverage, we build that into the role up front.

We screen for experience with the major field service platforms and train on your specific setup during onboarding.
